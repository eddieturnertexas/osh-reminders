A cancelled appointment counts as needing one. That is a judgment call, and it is worth 58 patients. The consent cut happens upstream in silver, so bronze keeps all 2,000 and the filter is not re-litigated here. No patient was lost to a missing phone number.

2

Rank the pool

One ROW_NUMBER() over five sort keys, applied in order. The first key that differs decides.

  • Risk — Highest/VIP, then High/Serious, then Medium/Moderate. The brief’s core requirement.
  • Need — no appointment ahead of cancelled.
  • Newness — new_vip ahead of initial, so a newly flagged VIP is not stuck behind the original list.
  • Overdue — oldest last_pcp_visit_date first. The longest without care goes first.
  • Id — deterministic tiebreak, so the same input always produces the same plan.

Because ranking runs over whoever is eligible right now, anyone who opted out or booked an appointment since the last run simply is not there and the ranks close up behind them. Backfill needs no separate mechanism.

5

Spread across five weeks

The 20% threshold is 200 patients a week. Rank decides the week, so the highest-risk patients are contacted first.

-- 20% of 1,000 = 200 per week
CAST(ceil(priority_rank / 200.0) AS INT)          AS send_campaign_week
date_add(current_date(), (send_campaign_week - 1) * 7)  AS send_week_start_date
weekofyear(send_week_start_date)                        AS send_week_of_year

The week is derived from a date, then converted, rather than adding to a week number. Adding 4 to week 51 would give week 55; adding 28 days gives the right week in the next year.
